Symposium

A table of philosophers who argue aloud, in their own rhythms, and may cite only what they actually wrote. You are the host. This is how the evening works.

01

Lay a table

The app opens with empty chairs, on purpose — nobody is chosen for you. Three ways to fill them, and they are different offers rather than one offer three times.

tap a chair Opens the roster, searchable by name, school, position or century. Type stoic, or kier, or 1600s.
lay a table Fifty-four curated arguments. Each names the disagreement in a line, because that is the reason to pick one.
seat me a table A draw from all seventy-five. The second chair is always from a different tradition than the first, or the evening opens with two men agreeing.

Two to five chairs. You can seat someone mid-conversation with draw up a chair — he arrives into the argument in progress and answers that, not a fresh question.

02

Ask them something

Type it, or hold the microphone and speak it. Open the sentence with a name and it goes to that man first — “Marcus, does the fort hold?” — and the rest of the table decides for itself whether to join him.

something to ask them offers questions written for the table as it is actually seated — matched to who is in the chairs, not to which preset you last tapped.

03

What happens in a round

Everyone at the table is scored on how strongly your question touches what they care about. The strongest reaction leads. The man most opposed to him speaks second — friction is the design, not a side effect. A third joins only with a genuinely different angle.

Each contribution arrives in two or three turns: a short opening that states his answer, then the explanation. That is not a style choice — every turn is spoken the moment it is written, so a short opening reaches you while the rest is still being composed.

A name, spoken The narrator says who has the floor — once, on a change of speaker. Nobody reintroduces the man still talking.
A stage direction · laughs, · does not look up. Manner, never mind: what a person in the room would see.
A citation The work he is drawing on, tappable for the reference.
A turning mark His voice is being fetched. Words arrive first; audio follows.
04

Move the conversation

Four things you can ask for that a plain question will not produce.

hear one of them out One man, the floor, no interruptions — his whole position in four to six movements, the first spoken while the rest are still being written.
steelman the other case One philosopher argues another's position in good faith, marked with a laurel, then earns exactly one aside in his own voice. The man whose view it is may judge it.
a raised hand Somebody who was addressed and has not answered. Tap his portrait and take it.
excuse him Remove a man mid-sitting without ending it. Swap one for another and the newcomer arrives at the end of the arc — the table is a sequence of arrivals, not a seating plan.
05

Take the floor

Tap your own portrait and you have asked to speak. The man talking finishes his thought, nobody new begins, and the label under you goes waiting → your turn. Holding the microphone does the same thing and silences the room at once.

Pause Holds the line where it is. Play resumes at the same word.
Interrupt Abandons the line and everything queued behind it.
Mute Makes the table inaudible. The argument continues in text.

Three controls that stop sound and they are not the same. Pause keeps your place. Interrupt does not. Mute does not stop anything — it stops you hearing it.

06

Let them talk without you

For a sitting you cannot attend to — running, driving, cooking. Choose let them talk by themselves before you ask, then ask however you like. The table takes it from there and the toast closes it. Nothing needs tapping again.

It quickens near the close, whatever it started as, so the last round is the end of a movement rather than a sudden change of manner. And it stops early when the argument runs dry — a twenty-minute setting ending at eleven is the table having finished, not a fault. take the floor ends it at the next round boundary.

07

End it, or keep it

toast closes the sitting. The table finishes what it was already saying first — the button says letting them finish… while it drains — and then raises a glass.

back to the table Puts the glass down. The argument is intact; one more question is the most ordinary thing to think of after deciding you had finished.
keep the whole sitting Writes both a readable transcript and an archive the app can reopen.
save the voices An audio recording of the evening, your questions included.
kept sittings Reopen an evening and hear it again, line by line, in the voices that said it.

A rough copy is written after every round, whether you ask or not, as Unfinished sitting.txt in Files → On My iPhone → Symposium. It survives a crash. It is the words, not the audio, and the next evening overwrites it.

A reopened sitting is for hearing again, not for carrying on. The argument's own memory is not restored, and the screen says so rather than letting you find out.

What they remember

They have met you before

Each sitting is recorded as a gist and read back at the next one, so a philosopher can say “when you were last here you doubted…” — and will not reuse an image or an opening he has already spent on you.

He remembers only the evenings he was actually at. A man recalling a conversation he was absent from is inventing one, which is the same fault as an invented citation and harder to catch, because you may half-remember it too and agree.

A steelman is recorded as a case put, never as a view held. He argued it because you asked.

The rules they cannot break

Why the citations can be trusted

Every integrity rule here is enforced in code, not merely requested in a prompt. A turn that breaks one is dropped before it can be spoken.

Own library only A philosopher may name his own works, by exact title. Nothing invented, nothing borrowed from the man beside him.
Exclusions honoured Books he did not write, could not have written, or that were assembled after his death are refused — with the reason attached.
Quotation verified Exact words are permitted only where the app holds the text and can check them. Everything else is paraphrase.
Spoken work, never quoted A lecture or interview may be cited and never quoted verbatim.
Manner, not mind Stage directions describe what a person in the room would observe. Never what a man is feeling.

Fourteen philosophers can be quoted exactly today, because their texts are bundled: Marcus Aurelius, Plato, Aristotle, Hobbes, Locke, Hume, Descartes, Spinoza, Nietzsche, Darwin, Douglass, James, Tolstoy and Dostoevsky. For the rest, quotation is refused rather than guessed.

Two Simones, three Thomases

Calling a man by name

Where a given name is shared, everyone who shares it takes a surname initial — Karl M and Karl P, Simone W and Simone B, Thomas H, Thomas A and Thomas K.

The bare name reaches nobody, deliberately. Guessing between two men is worse than asking again, and you would hear the wrong one answer with no sign that anything had gone wrong.

Voices

Who sounds like whom

The phone's own voices work with no setup and no cost. Cloud voices are better and are billed per character; the server holds the key and enforces a daily budget, so the system voices take over rather than the table going quiet.

who is speaking Assign a voice per chair, set how they are pronounced, and tell them what to call you.
cloud voices Search the whole roster, see who still lacks a voice, and fill the rest without overwriting the ones you chose.

Give your name and they will use it — sparingly, once or twice an evening, at the moment a man turns to you. A name in every turn is the register of a hold queue and would undo the thing it was added for.

When something goes wrong

What the messages mean

Overloaded The service is busy. It is retried for you before you ever see this; when you do, wait a few seconds and use the button underneath.
let them finish The connection dropped part-way. What you heard stands; this asks for the rest.
ask them again The fault landed before anybody spoke. Re-poses your question unchanged.
System voices took over A cloud voice failed once. The words are unchanged. It clears itself, and you can tap it away.

You never have to leave a sitting to recover from a fault. Every transient failure offers the way onward directly beneath it.

One thing to know

The roster ships with the app

Philosophers, curated tables and bundled texts are compiled into the app itself. There is no database and nothing downloads in the background. Anyone added since your last install is not there until you build again — which is worth remembering the day a search comes back empty for a man you know exists.
